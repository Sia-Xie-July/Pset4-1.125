# Develop three distinct arguments for Before the Merge

Follow the snapshotted policy, project, decisions, accepted_evidence and schema.
Return only {"briefs":[Brief,Brief,Brief]} in English, IDs 01, 02, 03 exactly once.
No code fences. The working titles below are directions, not predetermined findings:

01 — More Agent Output, Better Delivery? What Changed This Month
Identify the recent change that matters most to a 3–10-person team. Explain a
workflow consequence, not a catalog of releases. Distinguish changed capability
from demonstrated delivery improvement.

02 — The Review Bottleneck: Which Productivity Claims Survive Scrutiny?
Compare the actual support for relevant claims. Explain whether differences in
tasks, review effort, evidence quality, or incentives explain apparent tensions.
Do not assert that a bottleneck exists or that agents are ineffective unless the
supplied evidence establishes it. Revise the title/argument to fit the evidence.

03 — Before You Expand Autonomy: A Small-Team Evaluation Plan
Derive a practical, reversible evaluation from the evidence. Identify suitable
tasks, acceptance criteria, human checkpoints, and what to measure. Label proposed
metrics/practices as our inferences, not measured benefits or a completed pilot.

Each brief needs a question, one distinct central argument, a synthesis that
explicitly connects multiple sources, material limitations/counterarguments, and
nonempty claims. Each piece actually cites at least three independent originating
publishers; the series actually uses feed, youtube, and podcast evidence. Do not
invent a contrary view or add weak claims to satisfy variety or source counts.

Scope claim IDs to their piece (01-c1, 02-c1, etc.). Use reported_fact, source_claim,
or inference accurately. Every claim needs evidence from that piece's accepted
set, with exact item_id/version_id and a valid locator. For written sources copy
an exact retained substring; for media use an existing segment_id. Never paraphrase
inside locator.quote, translate its text, supply a title as a passage, or invent
segment IDs. Explain inferential steps in synthesis. Narrow an argument if the
evidence cannot support a stronger one. Do not fabricate evidence to make three briefs.
